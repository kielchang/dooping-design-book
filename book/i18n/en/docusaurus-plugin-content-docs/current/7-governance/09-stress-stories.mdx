# Stress-Test Stories

## The problem

Demo data is polite: short names, small numbers, one status, and no missing values. A component
can look correct in that story and fail immediately with production-shaped data.

## One separate category for extremes

Keep stress stories separate from the everyday catalog. They are not a normal usage suggestion;
they are a focused exercise for finding layout, contrast, overflow, and interaction failures.

## Seven useful extremes

Test long labels, empty and missing values, very large and very small numbers, dense rows,
mixed statuses, slow or failed loading, and narrow/mobile widths. Give each category at least
one realistic case and one deliberately difficult case.

## Stress reveals rules

When a stress story exposes a failure, turn the lesson into a component rule, token constraint,
or automated regression. The value is not only finding a bug; it is making the next implementation
less likely to repeat it.

## When to run them

Run stress stories before release, after token or typography changes, and whenever a component's
content model changes. They are especially important for tables, charts, forms, and navigation.
